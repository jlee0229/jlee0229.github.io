Cosets vs irreps

When small networks learn a group's multiplication table, do their neurons organize around cosets of subgroups (Stander et al.) or around irreducible representations (Chughtai et al.)? In the models I tested, around cosets.

Main results

I analyzed 89 one-hidden-layer MLPs on 7 groups: 50 on S5, 8 on the dihedral groups D59 and D61, and 31 on the affine groups Aff(Zn) for n = 11, 15, 16, 21. Pre-registrations were frozen first. The test was whether neurons are organized by cosets of subgroups or by a single-frequency irrep basis.

  1. Neurons are organized by cosets. On S5, all 50 models put at least 90% of their pre-activation energy in coset-aligned neurons (median 100%); untrained networks score 0. On the affine groups, 3 of the 4 testable blocks passed as plain cosets and the fourth (n = 21) was unclear. The single-frequency basis never carried a majority in any block, even though it solves the task with less redundancy.
  2. Direct products get factored. Aff(Z15) and Aff(Z21) split into smaller groups; every model solves the factors separately and leaves the joint block empty.
  3. The coset circuit sets the width threshold post hoc On Aff(Fp) the hand-built coset circuit needs about p² neurons, and grokking starts near that width. At p = 11, 0 of 3 seeds grok at width 100 and 3 of 3 at width 140. At p = 13, all 6 runs fail at width 128 and every run at 160 or above succeeds.
  4. ReLU matters post hoc With |t| the networks build the same coset circuit. With a quadratic activation the coset preference disappears, and the networks do not switch to the single-frequency basis either.

What failed or is open

Aff(Z13) never learns at width 128. The S5 memorizer control was the wrong null, which I recorded as a design error. Two pre-registered predictions on Aff(Z15) were inconclusive. All models are one-hidden-layer ReLU MLPs at width 128 under two optimizers, so the preference for cosets is a claim about this regime. The repository was pushed as a single commit after the work was done, so git history does not show the freeze dates; the dates inside each pre-registration file are the only record of ordering.
