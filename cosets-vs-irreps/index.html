Cosets vs irreps

A pre-registered experiment. Findings 3 and 4 came after the fact and are marked post hoc.

There are two published answers to how a small network learns a group's multiplication table. Chughtai et al. say its neurons line up with irreducible representations, and Stander et al. say they line up with cosets of subgroups. I analyzed 89 networks to check, and in the models I tested, it was mostly cosets.

What I found

The 89 networks are one-hidden-layer MLPs on 7 groups: 50 on S5, 8 on the dihedral groups D59 and D61, and 31 on the affine groups Aff(ℤn) for n = 11, 15, 16 and 21. I froze the pre-registrations first. Each one asks whether the neurons are organized by cosets of subgroups or by a single-frequency irrep basis.

  1. Neurons are organized by cosets. On S5, all 50 models put at least 90% of their pre-activation energy in coset-aligned neurons, with a median of 100%, and untrained networks score 0. On the affine groups, 3 of the 4 blocks I could test passed as plain cosets, and the fourth (n = 21) was unclear. The single-frequency basis never carried a majority in any block, even though it solves the task with less redundancy.
  2. Direct products get split up. Aff(ℤ15) and Aff(ℤ21) break into smaller groups, and every model solves the pieces separately and leaves the joint block empty.
  3. Post hoc. The coset circuit sets the width threshold. Built by hand on Aff(𝔽p), the coset circuit needs about p2 neurons, and grokking starts near that width. At p = 11, 0 of 3 seeds grok at width 100 and all 3 do at width 140. At p = 13, all 6 runs fail at width 128, and every run at width 160 or above succeeds.
  4. Also post hoc. ReLU matters. With |t| as the activation, the networks build the same coset circuit. With a quadratic activation the coset preference disappears, and the networks don't switch to the single-frequency basis either.

What didn't work, or isn't settled

Aff(ℤ13) never learns at width 128. The memorizer control I used for S5 was the wrong null, and I recorded that as a design error. Two of the pre-registered predictions for Aff(ℤ15) came out inconclusive. Every model is a one-hidden-layer ReLU MLP at width 128, trained with two optimizers, so the coset preference is a claim about this setting only.

I pushed the repository as one commit after the work was done, so git history can't show when I froze each pre-registration. The only record of the order is the date written inside each pre-registration file.
